# How to capture an invoice — Diedericks Dobermanns

For whoever enters expenses. Read this once; after that it is four questions per line.

---

## Why we split invoices at all

Because we want to answer three questions that a shoebox of receipts cannot:

1. **What does each dog cost us?** — so we can compare bitches against bitches, and know which
   dogs earn their keep.
2. **What did that litter cost, against what it earned?** — real profit per litter, not a guess.
3. **What does the business cost to run**, separately from the animals?

If every invoice goes in as one lump, all three answers are unavailable forever. That is where we
have been: **349 of 351 expenses are filed as "general"**, which means unanalysable.

---

## The one question that decides everything

For **each line on the invoice**, ask:

> **Who or what consumed this?**

There are only four possible answers, and every line is exactly one of them.

### 1. COMPANY — the business consumed it

Overhead that would exist whether we had four dogs or forty.

> office supplies · accounting and legal fees · bank charges · website and software · marketing ·
> kennel repairs and maintenance · tools · general cleaning materials · insurance on the property

**Never appears in any dog's cost or any litter's cost.** That is the whole point of the category —
it keeps the per-dog figures honest.

### 2. DOG — one named animal consumed it

> a vet consult for Hunter-King · Cleopatra's DNA test · Santini's import costs · a specific dog's
> training fees, entry fees, or registration

**Pick the dog.** If it is genuinely for two dogs, split it into two lines.

### 3. LITTER — a whole litter consumed it

> stud fee · progesterone testing · the C-section · whelping box and heat lamp · the puppies'
> first vaccinations and dewormings · microchips for that litter · registration of the litter

**Pick the litter.** These are the costs that only exist because that litter exists.

### 4. SHARED — the dogs on the property consumed it, and you cannot say which

> dog food · bedding · kennel staff wages · general vet retainer · flea and tick treatment bought
> in bulk · transport to and from the vet

**This is the big one, and it is why the old system never worked.** Nobody is going to split a
R14,000 feed invoice across four litters by hand every month — so it never got split, and
cost-per-dog never existed.

**Now you do not have to.** Choose SHARED and the system splits it across the dogs that were on the
property on that invoice date, weighted so a puppy counts less than an adult and a nursing dam
counts more. Check the list it shows you, adjust if something is obviously wrong, save.

## A worked example

**Agrimark invoice 17702 — 28 Aug 2026 — R14,880.00**

| Line | Amount | Kind | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dog food, 8 × 20kg | R11,200 | **SHARED** | every dog eats it |
| Vet consult — Hunter-King | R1,450 | **DOG** → Hunter-King | one named animal |
| Whelping box + heat lamp | R1,680 | **LITTER** → Claire × Santini | only exists for that litter |
| Brooms, hose fittings | R550 | **COMPANY** | kennel upkeep, not an animal |
| | **R14,880** | | **must equal the invoice total** |

One invoice. One attachment. One VAT reconciliation. Four honest destinations.

## Rules that keep the books trustworthy

**The lines must add up to the invoice total.** The screen shows the running difference. If it does
not balance, something was mistyped — find it rather than forcing it.

**Enter the invoice as it is printed.** Do not merge lines to save time, and do not invent lines
that are not on the paper. The invoice is the evidence; our records must match it.

**Attach the invoice or till slip, always.** A number with no document behind it cannot be defended
to SARS or to an accountant.

**When unsure between DOG and SHARED, choose SHARED.** It is the more honest answer. Loading a
single dog with a cost the whole kennel consumed makes that dog look unprofitable and quietly
distorts every breeding decision that follows.

**When unsure between COMPANY and SHARED, ask:** *would we still buy this with no dogs on the
property?* Yes → COMPANY. No → SHARED.

**A puppy's vaccinations belong to the LITTER, not to the puppy** — until it is sold. After it
goes home, anything we spend on it is DOG.

**The dam's food during nursing is SHARED, not LITTER.** She is a dog on the property; the system
already weights her higher while she is feeding. Do not double-count her by also putting her food
on the litter.

---

## What to do when it does not fit

Some invoices are genuinely awkward. A few that will come up:

- **A bulk buy that will last months.** Enter it on the date it was bought, SHARED. We are not
  running accruals; the month-to-month variation evens out and the simplicity is worth more.
- **A vet visit covering three dogs on one invoice.** Three DOG lines if the invoice itemises them.
  One SHARED line if it does not — do not guess at the split.
- **A cost for a dog that has been sold.** Still DOG, against that dog. It is part of what that dog
  cost us in total, and the reports handle the date.
- **Something genuinely unclear.** Put it in COMPANY and add a note. COMPANY is the safe default —
  it never contaminates an animal's figures — and a monthly review can move it.

---

## Checking your work

At the end of the month, open **Finance → Reconciliation**. It shows:

```
  Total expenses      =   COMPANY + DOG + LITTER + SHARED
```

They must be equal. Anything that does not balance, or any invoice with no lines, appears as an
exception with a link straight to it.

**If COMPANY is a large share of the month, something is mis-filed.** We are a breeding and training
operation — most of what we spend should end up attached to animals. A big COMPANY number is not a
problem with the business; it is a signal that lines went into the safe default and need a second
look.
